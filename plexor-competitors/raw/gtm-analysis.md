## 1. Competitor Go-to-Market Analysis

### Labib Edu's Acquisition Flywheel
* **Primary Channel: In-Person Library Display & School Proximity Marketing:**
  * Labib places physical printed acrylic stands and posters in university copy centers and high school-adjacent stationery shops.
  * Libraries act as physical billboards and commission-driven sales reps.
* **Secondary Channel: Teacher Personal Social Media:**
  * Contracted teachers film 2-minute teaser videos for Facebook and Instagram directing students to download the app and buy codes at designated libraries.
* **Sales Motion:** 100% self-serve B2C once the student has bought the scratch card.
* **Saturated Channels:** Facebook feed advertising is saturated and suffers from low trust.
* **Under-leveraged Channels (Whitespace for Plexor):** Direct B2B institute onboarding (offering institutes their own white-labeled app) and Telegram Bot on-ramps.

## 3. Strategic & Growth Signals

1. **Labib Edu Expansion Trajectory:**
   * Expanding from Baccalaureate into primary and middle school grades (Grades 4–9) to maximize customer lifetime value.
   * *Signal:* Labib is moving "downmarket" into general K-12 education, leaving the high-stakes Baccalaureate intensive exam prep wedge ripe for disruption by a specialized, high-rigor platform like Plexor.
2. **Payment Ecosystem Maturation:**
   * Syrian commercial banks and telecommunications operators (Syriatel, MTN) are standardizing merchant QR code APIs for Sham Cash.
   * *Signal:* Digital payment friction is dropping rapidly; within 12–18 months, mobile wallet adoption will overtake paper scratch cards in major urban centers.
