## 2. Pricing Psychology & Tactical Breakdown

### Labib Edu's Monolithic Bundling & Opacity Strategy
* **Opaque Pricing ("Call for Price"):** Prices are not published publicly on their website. Students must register in-app or message WhatsApp support to receive current quotes. This allows Labib to adjust prices dynamically for inflation without backlash.
* **Forced Bundling Trap:** Labib packages all subjects into a single heavy purchase. While this maximizes initial average order value (AOV) for Labib, it alienates students who only need help in 1 or 2 specific subjects, driving them straight into the arms of Telegram piracy.
* **Device Lock-in:** Tying accounts strictly to a single hardware device ID prevents credentials sharing, but causes severe customer frustration when a student replaces their phone or has device malfunctions.

### Physical Institutes' Cash Installment Model
* Parents pay millions of Syrian pounds in 3 to 4 painful physical cash installments directly to institute accountants.
* Institutes have no recurring digital billing; reconciliation is completely manual and paper-heavy.

## 4. Pricing Whitespace for Plexor

1. **Granular Micro-Purchasing (The "Unbundled" Advantage):**
   * Allow students to buy a **single subject** (e.g., Baccalaureate Physics for $10) or even a **single high-difficulty chapter** (e.g., Organic Chemistry for $3).
   * This matches the irregular, constrained cash flows of Syrian families and completely destroys the value proposition of piracy.
2. **Universal Platform Credits vs. Rigid Course Vouchers:**
   * Instead of printing 50 different scratch cards for 50 different teachers, partner libraries sell **Plexor Universal Credit Cards** (e.g., 5,000 / 10,000 / 25,000 SYP).
   * Students top up their Plexor wallet and spend credits on any teacher or academy.
   * Libraries love this because their inventory risk drops from 50 SKUs to 3 standardized cards.
