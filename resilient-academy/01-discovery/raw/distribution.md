## 1. Distribution Channels Analysis

### Channel 1: Star Teacher Direct-to-Student Funnel (Primary Viral Engine)
* **Mechanics:** In Syria, students don't follow generic platforms; they follow **teachers**. When a renowned Baccalaureate Math or Physics teacher announces: *"My 2026 revision course and weekly mock exams are exclusively hosted on Plexor. Pick up your access cards from Library X or pay via Sham Cash,"* hundreds of students onboard immediately.
* **Cost of Customer Acquisition (CAC):** Near $0. The teacher acts as the distribution channel in exchange for an 80%–85% revenue split.

### Channel 2: Physical Library & Copy Center Network ("Maktabat")
* **Mechanics:** Syrian high schools and universities are surrounded by dense clusters of copy centers and stationery shops where students photocopy notes, buy past exam papers, and recharge mobile credit.
* **Partner Incentive:**
  * Libraries receive an 8%–10% margin on every physical card sold.
  * Digital code generation: Libraries can print on-demand thermal receipt codes or hold pre-printed sealed scratch cards.
  * Libraries have zero financial risk: cards are provided on consignment or generated via a secure library portal upon cash receipt.

### Channel 3: Institutional White-Label B2B Partnerships
* **Mechanics:** Onboard 3–5 mid-sized private academies (*ma'ahed*) in Damascus and Aleppo.
* **Incentive:** The academy extends its student capacity beyond physical chairs, digitizes attendance, and monetizes students who cannot commute daily.

## 2. Geographic Phasing

* **Phase 1 (Months 1–6):** Damascus & Rural Damascus (highest density of private institutes and star teachers).
* **Phase 2 (Months 7–12):** Aleppo, Homs, and Latakia.
* **Phase 3 (Year 2):** Syrian diaspora students (Lebanon, Turkey, Jordan, Gulf) studying the Syrian national curriculum, monetized via regional international cards/remittance.
